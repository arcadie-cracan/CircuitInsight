CircuitInsight user guide

Symbolic circuit analysis driven by the simulator's own operating point. The tool solves the exact small-signal network your simulator linearized, keeps the parameters you choose as symbols, and prices every approximation it ever makes in measured dB.

Getting started

CircuitInsight needs two inputs: a CIN file carrying the circuit topology, and a psf results directory carrying the simulator's operating point. Open both with Open CIN + psf… or launch preloaded from Virtuoso – see Working from Virtuoso.

On open, the tool works for you before you click anything:

  • First light – the exact numeric transfer function is solved immediately and plotted against the simulator's AC reference, so the plot pane is never empty. This first result is the honest, unmatched model – it is not entered into History. The plot interaction buttons (zoom, pan, save) sit in a vertical strip to the right of the plots.
  • Matched pairs are suggested and applied – devices whose operating points agree on every significant parameter. If the applied matches move the model, the message strip says by how much, measured.
  • A keep set is pre-selected within the time budget, so your first Solve is symbolic.

The breadcrumb under the toolbar – 1 Open · 2 Match · 3 Choose symbols · 4 Solve – shows where you are; every step is a click target.

The workflow

The intended loop, for a student meeting a circuit or a designer checking one:

  1. Open the run. First light shows the exact response against the simulator's.
  2. Check the matches. Matched devices share symbols – that is what makes textbook cancellations happen in the algebra.
  3. Choose symbols to keep. Rank scores each parameter's effect on the band; tick the ones your question is about. Every keep set is exact – the unkept parameters become their exact operating-point values.
  4. Solve. Pick the result form: Exact, Simplified · full order, or Simplified · lowest order. The Summary leads with the designer numbers – A0, GBW, one formula per pole and zero.
  5. Refine. What-if sliders, History overlays, the Error tab, the Reduce bench – each answers one kind of follow-up question.

Session states. Everything you build in that loop – matches, keep ticks, band, tolerance strategy and budgets – is autosaved beside the CIN (<name>.last.cistate) after every shown result and on close, with the computed solution riding along. Re-opening shows the truth first and offers File → Restore last state; Save state as… makes named checkpoints ("pm-study", "cmrr-band"). Selections always restore. The stored solution restores only when its fingerprint matches the run now open – the same data files, cap model, matches and tool version; anything changed and the state declares the solution stale and asks for one Solve instead of showing a result the current data cannot back. Losing a carefully built keep set to a crash, a close, or a cap-model toggle is the failure this removes. The Solve * marker is the companion rule inside a session: whenever the ticked keep set differs from the result on screen, the button says so.

The benches

The tool dropdown in the toolbar selects the analysis. Each bench shows its own controls and its own views; Summary, Expression and Error are views of any result and stay available everywhere. The dropdown offers only the benches the imported run can ground in simulator data: the loop-analysis family (Loop gain, Compensate, GFT) appears when the run carries stb results, Modes when it also declares two iprobes. A run without stb data can still open the loop benches through Analysis → Declare AC as return ratio: an explicit statement that v(net)/v(input) in the AC sweep is the loop gain, which then serves as their ground-truth overlay. A reconstructed loop quantity is never shown without simulator truth beside it.

Transfer

The voltage transfer from the selected source to the selected net. One Solve button; the form selector states the contract:

Exact H(s) with every kept symbol – no approximation of any kind.
Simplified · full order shortens the formula, keeps the shape. Every pole and zero stays; additive terms inside the coefficients are pruned until the response would move by more than the tolerance. The working model – safe to manipulate and valid at every frequency, with the exact structure still in it.
Simplified · lowest order the fewest poles your band and tolerance allow. Reactances are removed and added back by matching pursuit until the smallest set the search can certify remains – the presentation model you would draw on a whiteboard: A0, GBW, done. Certified only over the band you select.

The two answer different questions. Measured on a 17-pole folded cascode at a 1 dB tolerance: full order returned the same degree 17/17 with half the terms at 0.00 dB; lowest order returned degree 1 – A0/(1+s/p1) – at 0.67 dB.

What appears when. Each control exists exactly when it means something. Exact shows neither tolerance nor band. Full order shows the dB/degree collapse budgets and a fixed tolerance tube. Lowest order shows the tolerance strategy dropdown, its strategy's own spins, and the band slider.

The strategy states what is judged; the band states where. A lowest-order result is certified only over a frequency band – outside it, it is not slightly off but structurally wrong: everything past the last kept pole is fiction. Each strategy gates the reduction on the quantity its designer actually reads:

Gain & phase the spec-sheet contract: |H| within the dB budget AND phase within the degree budget at every band point. Trusts the cursor literally – a band dragged deep into the rolloff demands fidelity there too.
Stability (margins) the reduced model must reproduce the full model's phase margin (within the ° budget), gain margin (within the dB budget) and unity-crossing frequency. The band's only job is to contain the crossover; everything else is forgiven. The result reports the margins themselves: "PM 85.2°→85.0° (Δ4.9°), fc 12.7 MHz". With no unity crossing in the band it says so instead of inventing margins to preserve.
Rejection (dB) for CMRR/PSRR studies: the dB curve tracks the full model within the budget at every band point, phase unconstrained – dB error is relative error of the small quantity.

One contract for every approximation. The tolerance strategy, its budgets and the band selector define the session's accuracy contract – and every budgeted step speaks it: the lowest-order solve, the AC-ground scan, the removal scan and the applied reduction are all priced and gated by the same criterion over the same band, reported as a fraction of the same budget (0.42× budget; at or under 1.00× means within it). Exact steps say so: exact, no budget spent. After each solve the Summary appends the approximation ledger: each step's score, and the totals measured end to end against the imported circuit – never summed, because errors do not add linearly. The ledger is what tells you when a composition of individually-passing steps has exceeded the contract.

Every strategy is capped at a readable order: if the tolerance is not reachable with six reactances, the tool does not dump a 26-element model – it returns the best readable one with its achieved numbers and the remedy (narrow the band or relax the tolerance). An unreadable model is a failed answer, not a result. The tolerance tube shades each strategy's own promise: both budgets across the band for Gain & phase, the phase around the crossover for Stability, the magnitude alone for Rejection. The reported error is always measured against the full model, never assumed.

The order certificate. While you drag the band, a label beside the slider reports what the band demands: "lowpass band needs order 2 at 5%" – computed from the response samples (Loewner rank) before any reduction runs. Watch it as you widen the band: the knee tells you where the response's complexity lives. After a solve, the note says whether the physical model reached that order ("order-optimal for this band") or paid extra for named components. When the certificate's low order comes from a near-cancelling pole/zero pair, the note names the doublet – doublets are invisible in the frequency response but govern settling, so a band-certified model can be transient-blind; the caveat is part of the certificate. The full-order form keeps every pole, so it needs no band – it is certified over the whole working range.

Loop gain

The Tian loop gain at a current probe – any vsource in the design qualifies; a run carrying an stb analysis opens here with its designated probe selected. The plot annotates the phase and gain margins; the probe-adequacy advisor grades whether this probe's T actually predicts closed-loop stability, and its verdict lands in the message strip a few seconds after the solve.

Compensate

Suggests compensation networks that cannot move the operating point – capacitors and series-RC branches – sized to a goal:

  • mfm – place the dominant closed-loop pair at Butterworth damping;
  • pm – meet a phase-margin floor;
  • spec – hold the peak sensitivity Ms = max|1/(1−T)|.

Achievers are ranked by area – total added capacitance first. Selecting a row previews its loop gain instantly; branches above 1 grow a nested network one branch at a time.

Modes

For fully differential designs: the 2×2 differential and common-mode loop matrix, its eigenloci, and the per-locus margins. The run's own stb curve overlays the matching locus wherever it exists.

GFT

The general feedback theorem quartet for one loop: the ideal closed loop, the loop transmission, and the two error terms, with the exact identity checked and badged. Needs a probe and an error-reference designation.

Impedance

Z at a port – any isource or vsource marks one. A run carrying an xf analysis overlays it as truth.

Reduce circuit

The modelling workshop, and the one bench whose actions change the working circuit. The session holds one circuit in one of two named states – as imported or reduced – and every result records which state produced it.

  1. Scan AC grounds prices every mirror and bias node by the exact error grounding it would cause in the current transfer. The recommended set is pre-ticked; ticking any combination re-prices it jointly.
  2. The preview shows what grounding unlocks, both exact: the controlled sources that die because their controlling voltage became zero, and the parallel passives that lump into single Cnode and Rnode symbols.
  3. Apply reduction rewrites the working circuit and banners the true end-to-end cost, measured over the full band – grounding is the only approximation in the chain. Revert restores as-imported at any time.
  4. Scan removals is advisory: every explicit element priced by the response shift its outright deletion would cause. Act on it in the schematic, not here.

Reduced results carry a [reduced] marker in History, so an overlay against an as-imported result shows exactly what the modelling step cost.

Devices and matched pairs

The circuit lives in two trees. The Instances tree groups every reconstructed device under its subcircuit path – one clean column, with the details where you ask for them: hover an instance for its operating-point glance (type, region, terminals, gm = …, gds = …, voltages), double-click for the full record, right-click to set its LaTeX alias (per-symbol LaTeX stays in the keep table and overrides it). Devices outside saturation are tinted, and devices whose impact-ionization current the model cannot represent are flagged – their loop-gain results may differ from the simulator's. The Nets tree is hierarchical the same way: the testbench's own nets first, then each subcircuit as a node holding its internal nets, each net expanding to its connections. The input source's net wears ▶, the output net wears ◀, ground nets are grayed, and a net AC-grounded by the active reduction wears ⏚ – the tree always tells the truth about which circuit the analyses run on. Double-click a net to make it the output; right-click to route an AC-ground request through the measured Reduce flow – nothing is ever grounded silently.

Matching declares devices to share one set of symbols. It is what lets gm1/(gds2+gds4) emerge instead of six unrelated symbols – and it means one value set must stand for the group. Every member of a match set wears 🔗 with its group number and the group tint; right-click a linked member to unmatch the group or choose its representative. The values selector chooses how the group gets its numbers:

weighted – the default the mean weighted by each member's band sensitivity. The member that shapes the response most pulls hardest; measured best on every bench.
representative one member's values stand for the group. Right-click a 🔗 member to choose; with no choice made, the first device the netlist reaches wins.
average the arithmetic mean, parameter by parameter.

Whenever applied matches fuse devices whose operating points disagree, the message strip reports the worst overwrite and the measured response shift. If the plot disagrees with the simulator reference, look there first – then Unmatch the offending group, or Clear all matches.

The device model lives in the Model menu. Charge-matrix caps picks the exact trans-capacitance matrix over the five-cap lumped model. Lump ĝm = gm + gmb bundles the body-effect transconductance into the main one, exactly and per device: a device qualifies when its gate and bulk sit at the same AC potential (the same net, or nets held by DC sources), because there gm·vgs + gmb·vbs collapses to one term. Such a device shows a single hatted symbol ĝm whose value is the sum; a device with bulk tied to source has vbs = 0, so its inert gmb symbol is simply dropped. Devices with a signal-driven gate keep their separate gmb – lumping there would change the response, and this tool does not approximate silently. The strip reports exactly which devices were bundled; if none qualify, it says so. Selecting one of the justifying DC sources as the input is refused with an explanation rather than answered wrongly. Both model choices re-open the run, keep your matches and keep ticks, and re-run the last analysis.

The toggle composes with the Reduce-circuit bench: on a real extracted circuit a cascode gate usually sits on an internal bias net – not exactly AC ground, so no hat. Once the measured AC-ground flow grounds that net (with the response shift quantified against your budget), the criterion holds on the reduced circuit and the device earns its ĝm there – the grounding is the only approximation, already accounted for by the reduction's own error report; the bundle itself stays exact. The log names each device that gained its hat through the reduction.

The keep set and the estimate

The keep table decides which parameters stay as letters. Two facts keep it honest:

  • Every keep set is exact. Keeping fewer symbols loses no accuracy – the rest become the exact rational values of their operating point. Simplify's budget is the only accuracy knob.
  • Keeping more symbols costs time, multiplicatively – the solve grid is a product over kept symbols. Rank orders parameters by their effect on the band; Suggest picks the largest set fitting the time budget; the estimate under the table predicts the solve and is checked against reality on every run.
  • Suggest is form-aware. With lowest order selected it proposes the story keep instead: the reactances the reduction will keep – keeping them also protects them from being dropped – plus the few conductances that set A0 and the dominant pole over your band. Those are the only letters the whiteboard formula can carry; anything more multiplies the solve grid without appearing in it.

Reading results

  • Summary leads with the designer numbers – A0 with its formula when one exists, GBW, one short formula per pole and zero with the displacement each accepted – then the exact record. Analysis → Attribute poles adds which element establishes which pole, verified by nudging the owner and re-rooting. Analysis → Explain the numbers answers the follow-up question a simplified expression raises: what is inside a numeral such as the 4.8×10−5 multiplying a kept symbol? Every unkept parameter is substituted before the solve, so those numbers are sums of products whose names are gone; the advisor ranks them back per coefficient of N(s) and D(s), grouped by family – the gm chain that scales every term, then the gds pair or capacitance that actually sets the value. The Summary leads with the ratio attribution: a displayed numeral is always a ratio of two coefficients, and shares subtract in a ratio, so the common chain cancels and what remains is what shapes the number you see. The same attributions appear when you hover a numeral in the Expression tab. Shares are exact fractions for once-stamped parameters; the current keep set is excluded, since those are already letters. When a formula line carries SEVERAL numerals, their split is not resolvable at the operating point – Analysis → Explain per numeral runs the deep pass, a derivative sweep over the hybrid grid that resolves every collapsed numeral (coefficient of sk times each kept-letter monomial) individually. It costs a few multiples of the solve; afterwards each numeral's hover carries its own contributors.
  • Expression renders H(s) in publication form. Hover a symbol for its operating-point value; click it to select it in the keep table – and in the Virtuoso schematic when cross-probe is on. Hover a numeral for its ranked contributors once Explain the numbers has run; before that, the hover tells you to run it. LaTeX aliases are editable via the instance context menu and the keep table.
  • Log records the session as it happens: every run's start with its estimate, each phase transition with the elapsed clock, and a closing line giving the actual duration, how it compared with the estimate, and which solver backend ran. The status bar shows only the current line; this keeps the history, and it is the fastest way to report a slow or surprising solve – select and copy.
  • Error shows the residual of a budgeted result against the full model – two overlapping Bode curves cannot show a 0.5 dB difference; this tab can.
  • What-if gives each kept symbol a slider over ×0.25 – ×4; loop-gain results track their phase margin live. Disabled for lowest-order results, with the reason stated: moving a parameter can silently leave the band and operating point the reduction was certified for – explore parameters on a full-order result.
  • History records your solves; select several to overlay them on one Bode. First light and bench scans do not enter it.
  • Export writes a single-file report; Add view to report appends the current view to a session notebook, exported separately.

Working from Virtuoso

One line in .cdsinit installs everything – print the skill directory with circuitinsight-skill-path:

  CInGuiCmd = "/abs/path/to/circuitinsight-gui"
  load("<skill-dir>/cin_init.il")

A CircuitInsight menu then appears on ADE windows as they open – however they were opened – and on schematic windows via CInInstallMenu(). Analyze exports the schematic, finds the current run's results, and launches the GUI preloaded; the topology and the operating point always come from the same session. With cross-probe enabled in the Devices menu, clicking a symbol in the Expression view highlights its device in the schematic.

The exactness contract

Worth stating once, plainly. The reconstruction solves the exact network the simulator linearized. Symbolic results are reconstructions, not approximations – a solve that samples and rebuilds returns polynomials bit-identical to the symbolic determinant. The only approximations in the tool are the ones you ask for, and each carries its measured price at the moment you take it: a simplification budget, an AC-ground designation, a match across unequal operating points. When a result surprises you, the first question is which declared approximation moved it – the strip, the Summary and History overlays exist to answer exactly that.
